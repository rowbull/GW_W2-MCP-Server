# W2 Strategy MCP - Liquidity Introduction Prompt

Now that we understand your economic exposure profile, let's talk about your liquidity strategy - basically, how much cash and easily accessible money makes sense for your situation.

Given your [LOW/MODERATE/HIGH] economic exposure [and homeowner status], your liquidity needs are unique to you. 

Most people think about emergency funds in terms of "3-6 months of expenses," but that's pretty generic advice. We're going to look at this more strategically by considering:

1. **Your sources of liquidity** - what money you can actually access quickly if needed
2. **Your uses for that money** - not just job loss, but other scenarios where you might need cash fast
3. **How your economic exposure affects both sides** - someone with high exposure might need more liquidity, or access to it faster

The goal isn't to tell you exactly how much to keep in cash, but to help you think through a liquidity strategy that makes sense for your specific situation.

Ready to dig into the details?

---

## Context Variables for Personalization:

**Economic Exposure Levels:**
- LOW: "your relatively stable economic profile means you can probably be more efficient with cash"
- MODERATE: "your balanced economic profile gives you some flexibility in liquidity planning" 
- HIGH: "your high economic exposure means liquidity planning becomes more important"

**Homeowner Status:**
- Owner: "and homeowner status" → adds complexity to liquidity needs
- Renter: "" → simpler liquidity planning

## Transition Logic:
This prompt sets up the detailed liquidity analysis by:
1. Connecting economic exposure to liquidity needs
2. Explaining this isn't generic "emergency fund" advice
3. Introducing the sources vs. uses framework
4. Preparing for detailed liquidity inventory
