# W2 Strategy MCP - Job Sensitivity Prompt

## Logic: Read session_context for job information and categorize using economic sensitivity framework

### If we have clear job/company context:

Based on our earlier conversation, I see you work [at COMPANY/as ROLE]. Let's talk about how that fits into your overall financial picture.

[COMPANY/INDUSTRY] tends to be [CYCLICAL/STABLE/COUNTER-CYCLICAL] when it comes to economic cycles. What I mean is, when the economy hits rough patches, [SPECIFIC INDUSTRY BEHAVIOR].

For example, if you work at Google, tech companies often freeze hiring, delay projects, and see stock prices drop during economic downturns. If you're a teacher, your job tends to stay steady regardless of what's happening in the broader economy.

Does that match your experience? Do you see your job as pretty sensitive to economic ups and downs, or more stable regardless of what's happening in the broader economy?

---

### If we have vague context (e.g., "consulting", "finance", "business"):

I see from our conversation that you work in [INDUSTRY] - that covers a lot of ground. To understand how economic cycles might affect your situation, I'd love to know a bit more. 

What type of [consulting/finance/business] work do you do, and what kind of company or organization? This helps me figure out how economic cycles might affect both your job security and any company benefits or stock you might have.

---

### If we have no job context or it's unclear:

To understand your economic exposure, I need to know what you do for work. What company or industry are you in? This helps me figure out how economic cycles might affect both your job security and any company benefits or stock you might have.

---

## Economic Sensitivity Framework for MCP Classification:

**CYCLICAL (Rating: 3) - Sensitive to Economic Cycles:**
- Technology (Google, Meta, Apple, startups, software companies)
- Financial Services (investment banking, private equity, venture capital)
- Construction and Real Estate (developers, contractors, real estate agencies)
- Automotive (manufacturers, dealerships, suppliers)
- Airlines and Travel (airlines, hotels, tourism, travel agencies)
- Hospitality and Restaurants (hotels, restaurants, entertainment venues)
- Luxury Goods (high-end retail, jewelry, luxury cars)
- Non-essential Retail (fashion, electronics, home goods)
- Media and Advertising (marketing agencies, social media companies)

**NON-CYCLICAL (Rating: 2) - Relatively Stable:**
- Healthcare (hospitals, medical practices, healthcare systems)
- Utilities (electric, water, gas, telecommunications)
- Consumer Staples (grocery stores, food companies, household goods)
- Education (schools, universities, educational services)
- Government and Public Services (federal, state, local government)
- Pharmaceuticals (drug companies, medical device manufacturers)
- Insurance (health, life, property insurance companies)

**COUNTER-CYCLICAL (Rating: 1) - More Stable During Downturns:**
- Discount retail and thrift stores
- Debt collection services
- Bankruptcy and legal services
- Pawn shops and payday lending
- Repossession services
- Essential services (utilities, waste management)

## Confirmation and Storage Logic:

After user responds:
1. If they agree with the assessment → Store the corresponding rating (1-3) in job_economic_sensitivity
2. If they disagree → Ask "What makes you see it differently?" and adjust rating based on their reasoning
3. Store both the rating and capture their reasoning in the conversation for future analysis
4. Move to next step: investment risk assessment

## Sample Follow-up Responses:

**If they agree:** "Great, so we're aligned that your job has [high/moderate/low] sensitivity to economic cycles. This is important context as we think about the rest of your financial picture."

**If they disagree:** "Interesting perspective. What makes you see [COMPANY/INDUSTRY] differently? I want to make sure I understand your specific situation correctly."
